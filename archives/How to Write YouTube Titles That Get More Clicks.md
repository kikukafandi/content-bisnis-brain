Your title and thumbnail decide whether anyone clicks your video. YouTube's recommendation system can put you in front of millions of people, but a weak YouTube video title turns those impressions into scrolls.

There isn't one YouTube title formula that guarantees views. The goal is to make a clear, compelling promise that fits how viewers will discover your video, then test what actually works with your audience.

## What Makes a Good YouTube Title?

A good YouTube title clearly communicates what the video delivers and gives the right viewer a compelling reason to click. That reason might be a specific outcome, an unusual story, a useful benefit, an emotion, or a curiosity gap.

For search-driven videos, the title should also use relevant keywords so viewers can quickly recognize that the video answers their question.

Your title should work with the thumbnail, too. Together, they should communicate one compelling promise instead of two competing ideas.

## How Long Should a YouTube Title Be?

YouTube titles can be up to 100 characters long, but that doesn't mean you should use all of them. Longer titles can be cut off depending on the device and where your video appears on YouTube.

Aim to communicate your topic and main hook early. A viewer should understand why the video matters without needing to see every character.

## 9 YouTube Title Best Practices for More Views

### 1\. Match Your Title to Viewer Intent

Before writing a title, ask: **How is the ideal viewer likely to discover this video?**

Someone searching YouTube already knows what they want. A viewer browsing the Home page doesn't.

For a **search-driven** video, clarity wins:

"How to Edit YouTube Shorts on iPhone"

For a **browse-driven** video, the hook matters more:

"I Edited 100 Shorts. Here's What Actually Worked"

Many videos can attract viewers from both sources. The goal is to understand what your target viewer needs to see before clicking.

### 2\. Front-Load Important Keywords

If your video targets YouTube Search, put the phrase people are searching for near the beginning of the title when it sounds natural.

Think Media's ["How to Make YouTube Videos on Your Phone (Beginners Tutorial)"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=am7kaAerVCQ) follows that pattern: the searchable topic comes first, while the parentheses clarifies who the tutorial is for.

### 3\. Write the Way Your Viewers Talk

Every niche has its own vocabulary. Dog channels say "doggo" and "zoomies." Gaming channels use terms such as "speedrun" and "no-hit."

Using language your audience recognizes can signal that a video is made for them.

Beppo's video " [I Beat Every “Impossible” Minecraft Seed](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RGoQCy2BlMw) " uses "seed," the term Minecraft players use for a generated world, and tells the right viewers exactly what the challenge involves.

### 4\. Make Your Title Specific

Specific titles give viewers a clearer reason to click than vague promises.

For example: In the title "7 Editing Mistakes Killing Your Retention" adding the number gives it specificity, a than nd makes it more intriguing.

Jesse James West's ["I Ran Every Day for 30 Days, This is What it Did to My Body"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_bowCTqEp34) combines a timeframe with a concrete transformation. The number tells viewers how long the experiment lasted, while the final clause promises a result.

![](https://i.ytimg.com/vi_webp/_bowCTqEp34/maxresdefault.webp)

The goal isn't to squeeze a number into every title. It's to make clear what is distinctive about *this* video.

Use selective capitalization just as carefully. Emphasizing one important word can draw attention, as in **"Do THIS Before You Upload."** An entire title in ALL CAPS can be harder to scan and may look spammy.

If a detail doesn't make the promise clearer or more compelling, cut it.

### 5\. Use Emotion With Purpose

Emotion can strengthen a title when it reflects what viewers genuinely care about.

Words such as "easy," "brutal," "profitable," or "impossible" can add another reason to click when they fit the subject and audience.

Usually, one strong emotional word is enough. Stack too many together and the title starts sounding like an ad.

### 6\. Create Curiosity and Keep Your Promise Accurate

Curiosity can give viewers a reason to click, but a question isn't automatically a hook.

Compare:

**Can You Grow a YouTube Channel in 30 Days?**

with:

**I Tried Growing a YouTube Channel in 30 Days**

The second title promises a story and an outcome to discover.

Ed Progress creates a similar open loop with ["Do Eye Exercises Actually Work? I Tried for 30 Days!"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Djl07vKvP-I) The question names the uncertainty, while "I Tried for 30 Days" promises firsthand evidence instead of an unsupported answer.

![](https://i.ytimg.com/vi_webp/Djl07vKvP-I/maxresdefault.webp)

Whatever hook you choose, the video has to deliver on it. [YouTube prohibits misleading metadata](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2801973?hl=en), including titles and thumbnails that trick viewers about what they'll actually see.

Curiosity is fine. A broken promise is not.

### 7\. Make Your Title and Thumbnail Work Together

Your title rarely appears alone. On most YouTube surfaces, viewers see it next to the thumbnail.

Treat them as two parts of the same pitch. The thumbnail can grab attention and communicate an idea quickly, while the title adds context, specificity, or intrigue.

If your thumbnail says:

**I QUIT**

your title doesn't need to say:

**I Quit YouTube**

It could add the missing information instead:

**Why I Stopped Uploading After 5 Years**

Avoid repeating the same message in both places. Every word and visual should earn its space.

In jakkuh's 2026 video " [Why I left Linus Tech Tips](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gqVxgcKQO2E) " uses the thumbnail text "I QUIT." The thumbnail delivers the emotional headline, while the title adds the specific company and promises an explanation. Neither repeats the other.

### 8\. Use Proven Title Frames, Not Generic Formulas

Seasoned creators study titles that perform well, but the goal isn't to copy the words. It's to understand the promise behind them.

vidIQ [analyzed more than 4 million YouTube videos published in 2026 to see how different title frames were associated with performance](https://vidiq.com/research/youtube-title-formulas-study/). Some of the strongest patterns involved specific stories, including:

- An unusual event that was captured or witnessed
- A clear transformation
- Insider access or knowledge
- A warning about a mistake or risk

The broader lesson is simple: **specific story promises tend to be more compelling than generic formulas.**

For example:

**The Truth About Growing on YouTube**

is less specific than:

**I Posted 100 Shorts. Here's What Actually Grew My Channel**

## How to Test YouTube Titles With A/B Testing

YouTube Studio's built-in [A/B testing](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16391400?hl=en) lets eligible creators compare up to three variants on a video. You can test the title, thumbnail, or title and thumbnail together.

YouTube determines the winning variant using **watch time share**, not click-through rate alone. That means a title has to do more than attract clicks. It should attract viewers who keep watching.

### How to Run a YouTube Title A/B Test

1. Open YouTube Studio on desktop and go to the video's details page.
2. In the Title box, click A/B Testing and choose Title only.

## When Should You Change a YouTube Title?

Publishing a title doesn't mean you're stuck with it forever.

If a video is getting plenty of impressions but viewers aren't clicking at the rate you'd expect, its title and thumbnail may be worth revisiting.

Start with a hypothesis. Is the title too vague? Does it repeat the thumbnail? Is the strongest part of the video missing from the promise? Has the video's traffic shifted from Browse toward Search?

Then test a meaningful alternative instead of changing the title randomly.

Sometimes the best title can even change as a video's traffic sources change. That's the idea behind [vidIQ's Title Flip Framework](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/YouTube-Title-Flip-SEO-Framework/), where a video can shift from a curiosity-driven Browse title toward a more search-focused title later in its lifecycle.

## How to Title YouTube Shorts

YouTube Shorts titles follow many of the same principles as long-form videos, but you have less time and space to communicate the idea.

Three rules matter most:

- **Lead with the payoff or question.** Get to the interesting part immediately.
- **Use searchable language when it fits.** Shorts can appear in YouTube Search, so keywords still matter for search-driven topics.
- **Match the title to the Short.** Feed viewers can swipe away instantly when the content doesn't deliver what they expected.

Make the subject or hook clear as quickly as possible.

## Writing Good YouTube Titles Takes Practice

Strong YouTube titles are a habit, not a talent. Write several variations, make the promise specific, and test meaningfully different options when you can.

Research can point you toward stronger patterns, but your viewers ultimately tell you what works.

## FAQs

What Is the YouTube Title Character Limit?

The YouTube title character limit is 100 characters. Viewers may not see the entire title on every device or YouTube surface, so put the most important information near the beginning.

What Makes a Good YouTube Title?

A good YouTube title clearly communicates what the video is about and gives the right viewer a compelling reason to click. Depending on how viewers discover the video, that can include relevant keywords, specificity, curiosity, emotion, or a clear benefit.

Should You Put Keywords in YouTube Titles?

Yes, when the keyword accurately describes your video and reflects something viewers are searching for. For search-driven videos, placing the main keyword early can make the topic easier to recognize.

Can You Change a YouTube Title After Publishing?

Yes. You can edit a YouTube video's title after publishing. Make changes with a clear hypothesis and measure how viewers respond to the new packaging.

Can You A/B Test YouTube Titles?

Yes. Eligible creators can use YouTube Studio's A/B testing feature to compare up to three titles, thumbnails, or title-and-thumbnail combinations on eligible videos.

How Many YouTube Titles Should You Write?

Try writing at least three distinct title ideas before publishing. Explore different promises or angles, then shortlist the strongest options and test them when possible.
